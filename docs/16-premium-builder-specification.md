# Van de Stadt 67 ft — Premium Builder Specification Concept

**Status:** PROVISIONAL / DESIGN TARGET  
**Purpose:** builder-style premium specification for the future Polish Van de Stadt 67 completion.  
**Rule:** this is a target specification, not a statement of equipment already installed. All equipment must be checked against the actual drawings, weights, service envelopes, stability and electrical load study before final selection.

## Design brief
Premium 67 ft aluminium bluewater sailing yacht with superyacht-style systems redundancy, high hotel load capability, quiet operation, strong autonomy, simplified guest operation and full service access. Benchmark philosophy: Oyster 675 / Contest 67CS premium cruising specification combined with aluminium custom/expedition practice seen at KM Yachtbuilders/Bestevaer.

The yacht is to remain operable after failure of a single hotel-service component wherever practical. Critical services should not depend on a single AC source, a single freshwater pump, a single navigation display or a single sanitation-water source.

## Principal target arrangement
- 3 principal guest cabins: full-beam/maximum-volume Master, forward VIP, side guest cabin.
- 1 compact convertible guest/crew cabin with private ensuite.
- Convertible cabin: two separate single lower berths; one lower berth arrangement convertible into a double; additional folding upper berth which may remain stowed or deployed.
- Ensuite heads for all principal cabins and convertible cabin.
- Main saloon with panoramic deck-saloon character where permitted by existing structure.
- Dedicated navigation/technical control area.
- Dedicated machinery and electrical/service spaces with maintainability treated as a design requirement.

## Propulsion
- Main propulsion target: **300 hp marine diesel**.
- Final engine model: TBD after engine-room envelope, weight and shaft-line study.
- Heavy-duty marine reduction gearbox sized for continuous propeller load and yacht displacement.
- Flexible engine mounts plus thrust-management solution appropriate to final shaft arrangement.
- Stainless or equivalent approved marine shaft; final diameter by torsional/shaft calculation, not rule-of-thumb.
- High-efficiency 4-blade feathering/folding or controllable solution to be studied against sailing drag and 300 hp transmission requirement.
- Duplex primary fuel filtration with changeover while running.
- Dedicated day/service filtration strategy if tank arrangement warrants it.
- Water-cooled exhaust with high-capacity waterlock/separator and acoustic treatment.
- Engine-room forced supply/extract ventilation with temperature-controlled fans.
- Fixed automatic engine-room fire suppression plus manual release.
- Drip trays, service lighting, oil-change connections and remote service points.

## Generator plant
**Target: 2 × approximately 11.5–12 kVA compact marine diesel generators**, each independently capable of supporting essential hotel loads.

Preferred compact reference candidate for packaging study: **Fischer Panda Panda 12000x PMS**, approximately 705 × 450 × 590 mm and 195 kg in sound capsule. Final selection subject to duty-cycle, service network, acoustic, cooling and electrical review.

Architecture:
- Generator No.1 and No.2 independent starting, seawater intake/isolation, exhaust/water separation and protection.
- Automatic or controlled source transfer.
- Essential/non-essential load shedding.
- Ability to operate yacht normally on one generator with discretionary high loads managed.
- Shore + generator + inverter/charger sources integrated through monitored power management.
- Generator installation arranged for removal without destructive interior work.

## Electrical architecture
- 24 VDC primary service system.
- 230 VAC / 50 Hz hotel system.
- Dedicated engine and generator start batteries isolated from house bank.
- Large lithium house bank, final capacity by 24-hour hotel-load model.
- Dual high-capacity inverter/chargers in parallel/redundant architecture where practical.
- Isolation transformer / shore-power conditioning.
- Shore input sized after load study; premium target should support high-load marina operation without running generators where shore supply permits.
- Separate essential-services DC bus.
- UPS-backed network/navigation/control equipment.
- Central digital switching/monitoring with manual/local override for critical services.
- Tank levels, pumps, bilges, battery SOC, AC/DC loads, shore/gen state, HVAC, alarms and machinery status visible centrally.
- Energy meters by major consumers to permit real operating load analysis.

## Freshwater system — redundant premium architecture
Two independent pressure-water paths:

1. **24 VDC freshwater pressure pump set** — normal/emergency DC supply.
2. **230 VAC freshwater pressure pump set** — independent AC pump for high hotel demand.

Additionally, the 230 V pump shall have a **dedicated inverter supply from the 24 V battery system**, allowing the AC pump to remain available if the generator/shore AC bus is lost. The inverter is dedicated or electrically isolated so failure of the general hotel inverter system does not remove both freshwater paths.

Requirements:
- accumulator vessels;
- isolation/bypass valves permitting either pump to serve the vessel;
- duty/standby logic or manual selection;
- pressure sensors and low-pressure alarm;
- washable prefilters;
- hot/cold ring-main layout where beneficial;
- serviceable manifold system rather than hidden branch connections.

## Watermaker
- **150 L/h nominal reverse-osmosis watermaker**.
- Premium benchmark: Spectra-class 150 L/h installation or equivalent.
- Automatic freshwater flush.
- Product-water quality monitoring with automatic diversion until acceptable salinity is reached.
- Dedicated prefilter stages and service access.
- Product water routed to selectable freshwater tank(s).
- Remote monitoring at technical panel.

## Hot water
- Dual-source calorifier arrangement sized for guest complement.
- Engine heat exchanger plus 230 V immersion heating.
- Consider twin calorifiers for redundancy/distribution efficiency depending on longitudinal plumbing runs.
- Thermostatic anti-scald mixing.
- Hot-water recirculation where required to avoid long waits/water waste.

## Sanitation — dual flush-water concept
Each guest toilet to use premium electric marine sanitation hardware with a **selectable flush-water source**:

- freshwater from potable-water tank; or
- filtered seawater from dedicated sanitation seawater supply.

Both modes discharge toilet waste to the **black-water holding system**. Seawater selection changes the flush-water source only; it does not bypass the holding tank in normal guest mode.

System requirements:
- clearly labelled source selector / controlled valve logic;
- non-return and anti-siphon protection;
- no possibility of seawater cross-contaminating potable water;
- freshwater connection protected by proper air-gap/backflow strategy as required by final equipment and applicable standards;
- black-water tank level monitoring with high-level alarm;
- deck pump-out connection;
- controlled overboard discharge arrangement only where legal, with secured valves;
- macerator/discharge pump redundancy or emergency service provision;
- odour-resistant sanitation hose and vent filtration.

## HVAC
- **2 independent chilled-water chiller modules** rather than one large single point of failure.
- Each chiller isolated with valves so one can be serviced while the other remains operational.
- Staged operation according to hotel load.
- One **fan-coil unit per conditioned space/cabin** as the design rule, with independent thermostat/control.
- Dedicated fan-coil for Master.
- Dedicated fan-coil for VIP.
- Dedicated fan-coil for side guest cabin.
- Dedicated fan-coil for convertible cabin.
- Dedicated unit(s) for saloon/deck saloon according to heat-load calculation.
- Dedicated unit for galley/navigation/crew/service areas where required.
- Condensate drains engineered to avoid odour/backflow and inaccessible traps.
- Chilled-water pumps arranged duty/standby where feasible.
- Acoustic isolation of pumps, compressors and pipework.
- Heating capability via reverse cycle and/or hydronic backup to be studied for true four-season use.

## Entertainment and AV
Every guest cabin and main saloon to have TV provision.

Premium distributed audio architecture inspired by Sonos-style zoning:
- independent audio zone per principal cabin;
- convertible cabin zone;
- saloon zone;
- cockpit zone;
- optional foredeck zone;
- central rack-mounted amplification/network equipment rather than consumer units scattered through joinery;
- Ethernet backbone to fixed AV equipment;
- AirPlay/network streaming support;
- local volume/source control plus central integration.

Current reference technology: Sonos Amp Multi provides 8 independently configurable amplified outputs at 125 W/channel into 8 ohms; marine-rated speakers should be used in exposed areas. Final AV architecture should be integration-friendly and not dependent on cloud connectivity for basic local playback.

- **4 dedicated instrument displays** for sailing/navigation/machinery data;
- autopilot control head;
- VHF/DSC access;

- lighting/navigation-light controls;

Navigation sensor suite target:

Final display brand/model intentionally TBD until bridge geometry, sunlight, network and redundancy study.

## Steering and manoeuvring
- Twin helm stations.
- High-quality mechanical/hydraulic steering architecture appropriate to actual rudder arrangement.
- Autopilot drive with emergency bypass/manual steering retained.
- Retractable bow thruster strongly preferred.
- Retractable stern thruster to be studied against hull structure, sailing drag and weight.
- Thruster battery/hydraulic architecture selected after power study.

## Bilge and damage-control philosophy
- Multiple watertight/structural zones as existing design permits.
- Individual bilge detection in each relevant compartment.
- Automatic local bilge pumps plus high-water alarm.
- High-capacity central emergency bilge manifold/pump independent of small automatic pumps.
- Ability to use an engine-driven or independent high-capacity emergency pump to be studied.
- Manual pump independent of electrical system.
- Clearly accessible seacock manifold/labels and damage-control plugs.

## Fire safety
- Fixed engine-room suppression.
- Smoke/heat detection throughout accommodation and technical spaces.
- CO detection where combustion appliances/engine-space risk warrants.
- Portable extinguishers to class/flag plan.
- Fire blanket in galley.
- Remote fuel shutoffs and ventilation shutdown.
- Emergency lighting independent of normal hotel supply.

## Exterior/deck equipment target
- Electric/hydraulic powered sailing controls suitable for short-handed operation.
- Powered primary winches and furling systems.

- Dual-anchor strategy for bluewater operation.
- Washdown fresh/seawater at bow.
- Hot/cold stern shower.
- Powered passerelle/davit solution to be integrated after stern arrangement review.
- Tender handling/garage solution dependent on actual Polish hull geometry.
- Underwater lighting optional but consistent with premium finish.

## Noise and vibration target
Premium classification requires acoustic design, not merely sound insulation after installation:
- resilient machinery mounts;
- flexible exhaust and piping interfaces;
- acoustic generator capsules;
- engine-room multilayer insulation;
- isolated pumps/compressors;
- low-flow pipe velocities;
- fan-coil selection at low acoustic duty;
- no rigid pipe bridges into accommodation joinery;
- equipment located away from berth heads wherever possible.

## Benchmark evidence
- Oyster 675: ~21 m premium bluewater yacht; published specification includes 11 kW generator, hydraulic furling, retractable bow/stern thrusters, individual cabin climate control and integrated digital switching/monitoring.
- Contest 67CS: premium 21 m yacht; published/market specifications show 24/230 V architecture, dedicated technical electrical area, shaft/thrust-bearing arrangement, automatic engine-room fire suppression, 150 L/h Spectra watermaker, second freshwater pump for redundancy, chiller HVAC, three principal guest cabins plus crew/extra cabin and extensive domestic equipment.
- KM Yachtbuilders/Bestevaer: benchmark for custom aluminium yacht engineering, self-reliance, watertight integrity and owner-specific technical integration.
