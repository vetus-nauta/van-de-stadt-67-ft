# 03 — Propulsion, Shaft Line, Steering & Thruster

## No. 430 status

No confirmed original engine/gearbox/shaft/propeller specification for Van de Stadt design No. 430 has been located publicly. The current Gdansk hull is listed with diesel fuel type but no installed engine and zero rated power.

Therefore all machinery selection remains PROVISIONAL until displacement, engine-room geometry, shaft angle and intended motoring performance are fixed.

## Closely related 21 m Van de Stadt reference — Stadtship 70

Published equipment:

| Item | Stadtship 70 reference |
|---|---|
| Engine | Perkins Sabre M150Ti |
| Rated power | 148 hp / ~109 kW |
| Engine year | 2007 |
| Cooling | indirect / heat exchanger |
| Gearbox | ZF 45A hydraulic |
| Drive | shaft |
| Shaft | stainless steel, Ø50 mm |
| Propeller | 4-blade folding |
| Max speed | ~10 kn |
| Cruise speed | ~8.5 kn |
| Bow thruster | retractable, pneumatic powered |

**REFERENCE only.** The Stadtship 70 has a published displacement of 29 t, substantially lighter than the original 51 t No. 430 design. Its 148 hp engine must not be copied blindly to the 67 ft project.

## Propulsion sizing workflow for the 67

1. Establish actual design displacement at lightship, half load and full load.
2. Recover hull resistance data if available from Van de Stadt.
3. Define target continuous cruise speed and emergency reserve.
4. Determine propeller aperture and shaft angle from hull survey/drawings.
5. Select engine at continuous rating with realistic derating for hot engine room.
6. Match reduction ratio to propeller diameter and target shaft rpm.
7. Perform torsional/vibration calculation for engine–gear–shaft–propeller system.
8. Check shaft bearing spacing and alignment tolerances.
9. Design exhaust, ventilation, fuel return, controls and service access as one package.

## Preliminary power envelope

Do not freeze power yet. For a heavy 20.5 m cruising yacht potentially approaching the original 51 t design displacement, machinery should be studied over a broad range rather than assuming the 148 hp Stadtship 70 installation is adequate.

Candidate classes for study: roughly 150–250+ hp marine diesel, subject to resistance and propeller calculations. This is a PROVISIONAL study envelope, not a recommendation.

## Shaft line data required

- Engine bed location and width.
- Existing shaft-tube penetration, if any.
- Shaft-line angle.
- Stern-tube length.
- Propeller aperture diameter.
- Distance propeller to hull/rudder.
- Bearing positions.
- Required shaft diameter by class/ISO calculation.
- Coupling type.
- Flexible mount stiffness.
- P-bracket or skeg support if applicable.
- Shaft seal type.

## Propeller study

Compare:

- Fixed 3/4 blade — rugged, best reverse predictability, highest sailing drag.
- Folding 3/4 blade — low sailing drag; more complex.
- Feathering — strong manoeuvring, adjustable pitch on some systems; heavier/costlier.
- Controllable pitch — likely excessive complexity unless justified by mission.

Final diameter/pitch/blade area must be derived from engine torque, reduction ratio, aperture and displacement.

## Steering

No confirmed No. 430 steering gear has been found.

Design requirements:

- Rudder stock diameter and material from original drawing or physical measurement.
- Upper/lower bearing type and condition.
- Quadrant/tiller arm space.
- Mechanical vs hydraulic steering assessment.
- Autopilot ram independent mechanical attachment.
- Emergency tiller geometry and actual usability.
- Rudder angle indication.
- Redundant autopilot/drive strategy for offshore use.

### Reference philosophies

Van de Stadt 77 used twin cockpit steering wheels. A later Van de Stadt 84 example is documented with Lewmar Mamba mechanical steering and two outside helm positions. These are REFERENCES, not No. 430 data.

## Thruster

The Stadtship 70 reference uses a retractable bow thruster. For the future 67 study:

- Compare tunnel vs retractable unit.
- Determine bow immersion at light load.
- Calculate thrust from windage and underwater lateral area.
- Check installation against collision bulkhead and anchor locker.
- Decide electric vs hydraulic architecture only after overall energy system is chosen.
- Reserve cable/hydraulic runs before interior fit-out.
