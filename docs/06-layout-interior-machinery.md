# 06 — Layout, Interior & Machinery Spaces

## Confirmed current-hull accommodation

Current listing states:

- 3 guest cabins.
- 3 guest heads.
- Approx. 2.0 m cabin headroom.
- Deck-saloon concept.

No authoritative public GA drawing has yet been recovered for the current hull.

## Layout principles for future project

The future GA should be driven by structure, tankage and machinery access before furniture styling.

Priority order:

1. Watertight/structural bulkheads.
2. Engine room and major machinery extraction routes.
3. Tank boundaries and service access.
4. Mast compression structure and chainplate load paths.
5. Shaft line/rudder access.
6. Escape routes and emergency access.
7. Heads/plumbing stacks.
8. Galley and electrical service trunks.
9. Accommodation furniture.

## Machinery-space requirements

Reserve realistic service envelopes around:

- Main engine and gearbox.
- Generator(s).
- Fuel filtration/manifold.
- Batteries and main DC distribution.
- Inverter/chargers.
- HVAC/chiller plant.
- Watermaker.
- Water heaters.
- Hydraulic power pack if used.
- Steering quadrant/ram/autopilot.
- Black/grey-water pumps.
- Sea chests and strainers.

Every major component should have a documented removal path without destroying permanent joinery.

## Reference: Stadtship 70 interior philosophy

Published arrangement includes:

- 4 staterooms / accommodation for 8.
- 3 bathrooms.
- Owner stateroom forward with ensuite.
- Separate engineering area.
- Large saloon, chart table and sea-going galley.
- Two-cockpit deck arrangement.
- 2.75 m dinghy stored behind a transom hatch.

**REFERENCE only.** This is valuable as a space-planning precedent for a 21 m aluminium Van de Stadt, not as No. 430 original layout evidence.

## Candidate 3-cabin arrangement for study

Given the current listing's 3-cabin/3-head count, one plausible programme to test against the actual hull is:

- Owner suite forward or aft depending tank/shaft/machinery geometry.
- Two guest doubles/twins.
- Three ensuite/day-head-capable sanitary compartments.
- Raised deck saloon with panoramic glazing.
- Lower galley or deck-level galley depending desired social layout.
- Dedicated nav/office station.
- Proper machinery/utility room.
- Crew berth(s) only if operational profile requires them.

This is PROVISIONAL and must not be treated as an existing layout.

## Interior weight strategy

Because the original No. 430 displacement is heavy but the present hull is unfinished, interior fit-out presents a major opportunity to control vertical CG and final displacement.

Track separately:

- insulation,
- lining panels,
- floors,
- cabinetry,
- stone/solid-surface counters,
- glazing,
- upholstery,
- appliances,
- doors/hardware.

Avoid heavy materials high in the deck saloon unless accounted for in the stability model.

## Service trunks

Freeze continuous routes early for:

- main AC/DC cable trunks,
- nav/data backbone,
- HVAC supply/return,
- domestic water,
- black/grey-water hoses,
- hydraulic lines,
- fuel transfer/return,
- fire suppression,
- ventilation ducts.

Access panels should be planned as part of the joinery architecture, not added later.
