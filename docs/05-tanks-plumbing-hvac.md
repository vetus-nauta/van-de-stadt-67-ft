## Closely related Stadtship 70 reference

Published system:

- Fuel: 2 × ~500–550 L main tanks + 150 L day tank, total approx. 1150 L.
- Fresh water: 2 × 1500 L wing tanks + 500 L day tank; the wing tanks can also act as water ballast.
- Black/waste: 300 L.
- Watermaker: Spectra Newport 400, ~64 L/h.
- Hot water: 2 boilers, heated electrically and by engine coolant.
- Water pressure: pneumatic pressure-water system.
- Two air compressors for redundancy.
- Fuel day tank pneumatically supplied with emergency hand-pump backup.

**REFERENCE only.** The very large water capacity and water-ballast arrangement are unique design choices and should not be copied without naval-architecture review.

## Tank survey for the Gdansk hull

For every existing integral or fabricated tank identify:

- Exact position and boundaries.
- Internal dimensions/volume.
- Aluminium alloy and thickness.
- Baffles.
- Inspection openings.
- Fill, vent, pickup, return and drain fittings.
- Lowest-point drainage.
- Level-sender provision.
- Pressure-test status.
- Separation from hull shell where applicable.
- Service access.

## Fuel system design checklist

- Total endurance target.
- Main tanks and optional day tank.
- Dual Racor-type filtration/manifold philosophy.
- Supply/return line sizing.
- Tank cross-connect/isolation.
- Transfer pump + manual emergency transfer option.
- Water/sediment sump access.
- Deck fills and overflow/vent routing.
- Generator fuel supply isolation.
- Fuel polishing provision if long-range mission.
- Fire shutoff valves accessible outside engine room.

## Fresh water

Determine desired autonomy from crew/guest count and watermaker capacity. Include:

- Drinking-water tank segregation if desired.
- Pressure pumps, preferably duplex or serviceable redundancy.
- Accumulator.
- Hot-water storage.
- Thermostatic mixing.
- Dock-water inlet only with safe pressure regulation and anti-flood logic.
- Filtration/UV if desired.

## Black and grey water

The current hull listing confirms one black-water tank, but not its capacity.

Future design must establish:

- Each toilet discharge route.
- Holding-tank capacity by operating area/regulatory profile.
- Deck pump-out fitting.
- Overboard discharge pump where legal.
- Vent sizing and odor filter.
- Accessible anti-siphon loops.
- Grey-water collection philosophy: direct discharge vs sump/holding.
- Shower sump redundancy and cleanout access.

## Bilge system

Design as a separate safety system, not part of domestic plumbing:

- Multiple zoned electric bilge pumps.
- High-water alarms independent of pump controls.
- Manual bilge pump operable from safe location.
- Engine-driven or high-capacity emergency pumping option if offshore mission.
- Strainers accessible without dismantling furniture.
- Separate limber paths checked for watertight bulkhead strategy.

Stadtship 70 reference is documented with five electric bilge pumps distributed through the vessel.

## Watermaker

Reference capacity: 64 L/h on Stadtship 70. For this project calculate required production from actual consumption and desired generator runtime.

Questions:

- 60–100 L/h vs larger system.
- 230 V vs DC drive.
- Energy recovery membrane system.
- Intake location away from heads/engine discharge.
- Prefilter access.
- Automatic freshwater flush.
- Pickling/service arrangement.

## HVAC / heating

No confirmed No. 430 system is published.

Design inputs:

- Mediterranean summer cooling load.
- Winter northern-Europe heating requirement if relevant.
- Deck-saloon glazing solar load.
- Hull/deck insulation thickness.
- Cabin-by-cabin heat gain/loss.
- Engine-room ventilation.

Architectures to compare:

- Central chilled-water plant with fan coils.
- Distributed self-contained marine AC units.
- Diesel hydronic heating integrated with hot water.

For a 20.5 m deck-saloon yacht, central chilled water can improve zoning and machinery concentration, but introduces pumps, seawater circuit, larger pipe runs and maintenance. Freeze only after thermal-load calculation.
